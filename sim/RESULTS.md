# LOUD: 60-day simulation results

Generated by `node sim/run.mjs`. Do not edit by hand.

**This is model output, not market data.** The pool math and the three rule sets are computed by the rule engine
(`web/engine.js`), which is tested against the SPEC and does not model account registration, the 0.03 NEAR storage
payment, asynchronous receipts or `ft_transfer_call` refunds; how buyers and holders behave is assumed. The assumptions are listed at the end.
Each cell is the median of 9 seeded runs of 60 days at 1-minute steps.

Base parameters: SPEC standard preset: 1,000,000,000 supply, 5% creator, 100 virtual NEAR, 10 min launch window, 60 min exit window, 60 min cap window.
Early supply: the 5% creator allocation plus a 10 NEAR first-block bundle, 13.6% of supply in total, held in 30 wallets.

Columns:

- **max 1h drop**: largest fall of the spot price within any 60 minutes after the first 12 hours (median; worst seed in brackets). The launch hours are left out because a thin pool swings widely there under every rule.
- **early supply exit**: hours from the first sell attempt until the modelled seller has sold 99.9% of early supply. The LOUD seller goes through the notice and the cap and does not buy back.
- **announced (early)**: share of early-supply sales that were public at least one notice period before they executed. Under LOUD this is 100% by construction; under the baselines it is 0% by construction.
- **announced (all)**: share of all units sold into the pool during the run that went through the door (shout, notice, leave). It counts the early supply of the scenario and also ordinary buyers who bought inside the launch window, whose tokens are loud too. Every other sale is a quiet sale with no notice.
- **holder exit +1 min**: price an ordinary holder gets selling 0.01% of supply one minute after the first public sign of early supply selling (the first shout under LOUD, the first sale under the baselines), relative to the price just before it. This is a quote computed from the pool state at that minute. Nobody in the run makes that sale. If the pool cannot pay for the whole lot, it is the price of the part the pool can pay for.
- **worst holder exit in 30 min**: lowest such price during the 30 minutes after that sign (worst seed in brackets). Under LOUD this includes holders who rush out during the notice.
- **early supply proceeds**: NEAR received by the early supply.

## Which parameters the standard preset uses, and why

The standard preset is **cap 0.5% of supply per 60 minutes, notice 30 minutes, exit window 60 minutes** (SPEC section 3). In the day-1 bundle dump the baselines let the whole early supply out at once: the largest 1-hour drop is 44.6% with no rule and 44.6% with the per-wallet limit, which 30 wallets walk straight through, and the quote for a holder who reacts one minute later is 61.9% of the earlier price. With the standard preset the same supply needs 27 h to leave, the largest 1-hour drop is 19.5%, and the quote one minute after the first shout is still 100.0%; the worst within 30 minutes is 84.9%. A cap of 2% per hour with a 10-minute notice (loose) empties the early supply in 6 h with a largest 1-hour drop of 44.2%: in this model that is close to no rule at all. A cap of 0.25% per hour with a 60-minute notice (tight) gives a largest 1-hour drop of 8.9%, but the same early supply then needs 55 h, and honest early buyers wait in the same line. A 4-hour notice (long_notice) gives a largest 1-hour drop of 11.1% and an exit in 34 h; it mainly lengthens the time during which holders can leave before the first unit is sold. In this model the tight and long_notice rows fall less than the standard preset. The preset gives up part of that on purpose, for a shorter wait. The standard values are the ones written in the SPEC (section 3); the table compares them with tighter and looser variants. The preset keeps 0.5% per hour because a holder of 5% can still leave within half a day, and 30 minutes as a choice between two costs: a longer notice gives holders more time to see the board, and a shorter one keeps an honest early buyer from being locked in for hours. Whether 30 minutes is enough for a person to react was not measured.

## What the model does not show

- Where early supply does not dump, LOUD is worse than no rule in this model. In the calm launch and in the slow bleed the largest 1-hour drop is higher under the standard preset than under the baselines, and in the slow bleed the worst holder exit within 30 minutes is lower. The holder exit one minute after the first sign is higher. The cause is the assumed rush: a shout by an early buyer makes some holders sell. With the rush set to zero the calm launch falls less than the baselines (sensitivity table).
- In the slow bleed the insider sells far less than 0.5% of supply per hour on average, but it sells in daily batches of about 0.68% of supply, and that is more than the 0.5% the bucket holds. So the cap binds on each batch: part of the batch waits for the refill, and together with the notice the exit takes slightly longer (456.9 h against 456.0 h under the baselines). LOUD also makes each daily sale public one notice period ahead, and pays for that with the rush above.
- The differences between the LOUD rows in the day-1 dump also come mostly from the rush, which this model ties to the size of the first batch of exits. With the rush set to zero the standard preset falls far less (sensitivity table). Read the ranking of the variants with that in mind.
- LOUD does not protect the price level and does not stop early supply from being paid. The proceeds column shows what the early supply receives under each rule; LOUD changes when and how visibly it sells.
- In the late panic most of the fall comes from ordinary holders, whose quiet tokens no rule slows down. LOUD only keeps the early supply from adding to it in the same hour.
- The LOUD seller follows a fixed greedy routine (batches of exits shouted ahead of time). A better routine could exit somewhat faster. The seller does not buy back what it sold, so these runs do not show a holder who leaves, buys back quiet tokens and dumps them later (README, Limits).
- The rush (holders who sell during the notice) is an assumption with no measurement behind it. A larger rush makes the hours after a shout worse; the sensitivity table shows by how much.
- No arbitrage, no outside markets, no fees, no reaction of buyers to a visible board.

## Model assumptions

| name | value |
|---|---|
| insiderWallets | 30 |
| bundleNear | 10 |
| buyRatePerHour | 1 |

| launchDecayHours | 6 |
| buyMedianNear | 0.2 |
| buySigma | 1.2 |
| buyMaxNear | 5 |
| holdDays | 20 |
| panicThreshold | 0.1 |
| panicSlope | 20 |
| panicMax | 200 |
| boardThresholdBps | 30 |
| rushPerPct | 0.1 |
| rushMax | 0.3 |
| boardQuietHours | 24 |
| shockShare | 0.4 |
| shockHours | 2 |
| bleedDays | 20 |

Buy arrivals are Poisson with rate `buyRatePerHour + launchBuyRatePerHour * exp(-hours / launchDecayHours)`; sizes are lognormal. Each holder sells everything after an exponential holding time with mean `holdDays`, except that under LOUD the model keeps a loud balance or loud remainder below the shout minimum (one millionth of supply), although the contract accepts a shout of a whole loud balance that small; when the price has fallen more than `panicThreshold` over the last hour the sell rate is multiplied by `1 + panicSlope * (drop - panicThreshold) / 0.10`, at most `panicMax`. Under LOUD, when open exits first exceed `boardThresholdBps` of supply, each remaining holder sells with probability `min(rushMax, rushPerPct * board size in % of supply)` at a random moment within the notice period.
