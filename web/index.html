    The rule

    Buy at launch and your tokens are loud. Loud tokens sell into the pool in three steps, and the room can answer. Tokens bought later are quiet and sell at once. The times and cap figures in this section are the standard preset.

    1. Shout Say how much you are selling. The tokens lock and the exit is announced on chain.
    2. Wait 30 min Everyone can see what is coming.
    3. Leave within 60 min Sell into the token's own pool, as far as the cap has room.

    The room can answer. From the shout until the exit ends, any other account can catch it: pay for the tokens and take them. The price is the pool formula's price now or its price at the moment of the shout, whichever is higher. So a catch never costs less than the same share of what the pool would have paid for the whole exit when it was shouted. The seller gets 99% of it and 1% stays in the pool. The tokens stay loud. That part of the sale never hits the pool.

    The owner cannot cancel a shout. The tokens stay locked until they are sold, caught or, after the exit expires, returned by hush. The owner's own account cannot take them back sooner. A second account can, by catching the exit and paying the 1% fee. If they are not sold or caught, the exit expires 90 min after the shout, and they stay locked until someone calls hush.

    One cap for everyone. All loud sales into the pool share one cap. It holds at most 0.5% of supply and refills 0.5% of supply per 60 min. A hundred wallets get the same room as one.

    No insider list. The contract never decides who is an insider. It only tags launch-window buys. The creator allocation is loud from the start.

    The fine print
    • Loud means bought in the 10 min launch window, or part of the creator allocation. The tag sits on the tokens and moves with them.
    • The rule covers sales into the token's own pool. Other ways out are listed under Limits.
    • Only the shouted amount is locked.
    • A catch can take all of an exit or part of it, at any time from the shout until the exit expires. The price is the pool's sell quote for that amount at that moment, and never less than the same share of the quote at the shout. The payment to the seller is sent in the same call, less the 1% that stays in the pool. The cap is not used. A catch cannot leave less than one millionth of supply in the exit.
    • Whoever catches holds loud tokens and must shout to sell them. The exit owner cannot catch their own exit.
    • An expired exit holds its tokens until hush returns them to the owner, still loud. Anyone can call hush on an expired exit.
    • The cap starts empty. In the standard preset, at most 1% of supply can be sold into the pool as loud tokens in any 60 min, and 12.5% in any 24 hours. A catch is not counted: it moves loud tokens to another account.
    • A position smaller than the cap room leaves in one call once its notice is over, if the pool can pay.
    • Loud tokens cannot use sell or ft_transfer_call.
    • Nobody can pause, upgrade or re-tune the token. It has no owner method, and its account has zero access keys.

    How the run works
      • Standard parameters, with the creator allocation set to the chosen share and split evenly as loud tokens.
      • Thirty holders buy 300 NEAR of tokens when the launch window ends. That is minute 0. The insider first acts at minute 1.
      • With no rule or with per-wallet limits it sells all each wallet may, once a minute. Per-wallet limits: at most 0.5% of supply per sale, one sale per wallet per 60 s.
      • Under LOUD it shouts at minute 1 and first sells at minute 31. It shouts in batches and does not buy back. A sharper seller could be faster.
      • Nobody reacts in this run: no holder sells on seeing a shout, and nobody buys the dip.
      • The LOUD sale is still running when the chart ends. LOUD changes how fast and how publicly early supply sells into the pool. The price can still fall.
      • Same rule engine as the tests. It charges no storage; on chain every wallet but the creator's also needs 0.03 NEAR once.

        Limits

        LOUD slows one exit, the one into the pool, and makes it public. The rest is still possible.

        Leave, then buy back
        Once the launch window is over, a holder can sell loud tokens through the notice and the cap and, in the same transaction, buy the same amount back with NEAR of their own. The pool ends where it started, within rounding, and the holder now owns quiet tokens, which sell with no notice and no cap. So the cap sets how fast early supply can be sold or converted. It does not stop a later dump of tokens bought back at the market price. Every conversion still shows on chain as a shout and a leave.
        A catch is a cash-out the cap does not slow
        A caught exit sends its owner the payment in the same call, however large it is. The pool sells nothing and the supply stays loud, but an insider with a willing buyer cashes out faster than the cap. A holder can also take back their own shout by catching it from a second account, for the 1% fee. That makes a bluff cheap: shout, let others sell first, then take the shout back. Whoever catches takes on tokens they must shout to sell, while quiet holders stay free to sell at once. And a leave can fail with E_SLIPPAGE or E_NO_EXIT when someone catches from the exit just before it, so quote again and leave some slack.
        The seller sets the catch price floor
        The floor is the pool's price for the shouted amount at the moment of the shout, and it never comes down. A seller who does not want to be caught can lift the pool price for an instant around their own shout and so set a floor nobody will pay. After a real price fall, a catch can cost more than the pool pays, and then there is little reason to catch. Someone who sells just before a shout lands lowers the floor it records.
        A standing notice
        A holder can keep exits open one after another, so some exit is always ready. The notice then says a sale may come, with no exact time.
        Side deals and wrappers
        Loud tokens can be sent with a plain transfer to another registered account. That covers a private sale, and it covers a contract that issues its own token against them. Whoever buys that wrapper token holds no tag.
        Other pools
        ft_transfer_call refuses loud tokens. Quiet tokens can be sent with it. The receiving contract gets a fixed 30 Tgas for its ft_on_transfer, and if that is not enough the contract takes the tokens back, up to the quiet balance the receiver still holds.
        Waiting out the launch window avoids the tag
        Only buys inside the launch window are loud. A sniper who waits until it ends gets quiet tokens at the going price, with no notice and no cap. The creator allocation starts loud.
        Early honest buyers are loud too
        Anyone who buys in the launch window sells by the same rule.
        A shout is not a sale
        A holder can shout, sell nothing and get the tokens back once the exit has expired. The cost is gas and the lock, 90 min in the standard preset, or the 1% fee to take them back sooner through a second account. Until then anyone else can catch them, at a price that is never below the pool's price at the shout.
        A notice can start a rush
        Quiet holders can sell before a shouted exit is ready. That is what the notice is for, and it can speed up a sell-off.
        First come, first served
        No queue and no fair split. A holder who keeps calling leave can take every refill until the others' exits expire and they must shout again.
        Sybil resistance
        The cap is shared, so more accounts add no room under it. They do add exit slots, eight per account. The shared cap is the whole defence: the contract cannot tell people apart or split the room fairly.
        The creator allocation is unbacked
        It is minted without a NEAR payment, so every NEAR it takes out came from buyers. Once the creator has left, the pool may be unable to pay everyone else, and the last sellers can be stuck.
        Storage costs 0.03 NEAR per account
        Each account pays it once, on its first buy or through storage_deposit, and never gets it back. Only the creator is registered free.
        Not built
        Testnet only and unaudited. There is no fee on buying or selling, only the 1% on a catch, and no liquidity migration.
        Contract details
        When the pool cannot pay
        sell is refused (E_POOL_EMPTY), and leave fills only the part the pool can pay.
        What the storage payment covers
        The account's balances and eight open exits, the most one account can have. There is no withdraw and no unregister. A transfer to an unregistered account is refused (E_UNREGISTERED). Anyone can pay for any account.
        Tiny exits
        An account can shout a whole loud balance below the minimum size when it has no other open exit. So a tiny row on the board costs 0.03 NEAR per account. An exit too small to pay out cannot be filled and returns through hush after it expires.
        More than 100 open exits
        The contract then pages the list in storage order, and a reader can skip or repeat exits when the list changes mid-read.
        Failed payouts
        If a payout transfer fails after its call succeeded, the contract records the amount and the account collects it with claim. Nothing retries on its own, and the record is lost if that recording step itself fails.

        Fixed at deployment. Nobody can change them. Anyone can read them with get_params.

        Supply1,000,000,000Minted once. There is no mint method.
