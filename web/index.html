NEP-141 memecoin on NEAR testnet, built around one mechanic.

Early supply can leave. It cannot leave quietly or all at once.

LOUD limits how fast early supply can be sold on its defined path, no matter how many wallets it is split across. It does not prevent fraud.

The rule

The contract never decides who is an insider. It only knows when a token left the pool.

Tokens bought during the launch window, and the creator allocation, are loud. Everything else is quiet. Quiet tokens trade like any token. Loud tokens have exactly one way out.

  1. Shout Announce the amount. The tokens go into escrow and the exit joins the contract's public list of open exits, which the board reads.
  2. Notice, 30 min Nothing can be sold. Everyone can see what is coming.
  3. Leave, within 60 min Sell into the built-in pool, as far as the cap has room. After 60 min the exit expires and must be shouted again.

One global cap. All leaves together share one bucket. It holds at most 0.5% of supply and refills at 0.5% of supply per 60 min, so no 60 minutes can let out more than 1% (a full bucket plus one refill), and over a day the rate is 0.5% per hour. The bucket belongs to the token, so more wallets never mean more room.

  • The tag belongs to the tokens. Sending loud tokens to another account moves the tag with them.
  • Tokens sold through the door go back into the pool. Anyone who buys from the pool after the launch window gets quiet tokens. A purchase during the launch window is always loud, so where an exit can become ready inside the launch window (notice shorter than the launch window, as in the demo preset used on testnet), tokens that left through the door are loud again when bought in that window.
  • Loud tokens cannot be sold with sell and cannot enter other contracts through ft_transfer_call.
  • No owner, no pause, no upgrade method, no parameter setter. The token account has zero access keys.

What it leaves out. Nobody reacts: holders do not sell when they see a shout, and nobody buys the dip. Under LOUD the sale is still running when the chart ends, so its price line is an unfinished sale. LOUD does not protect the price level. It changes how fast and how visibly early supply sells.

Play the token by hand

A private copy of the rule engine that runs in this page without a wallet or a chain. You control the clock, and the errors are the engine's own messages.

  1. Buy with 10 NEAR. You are inside the launch window, so the tokens are loud.
  2. Try to sell. Read the error.
  3. Shout your loud balance. Try to leave at once.
  4. Skip 30 min. Leave. The cap fills part of it.
  5. Leave again. Skip time, watch the cap refill, leave again.

    Limits

    What LOUD does not do.

    Wrappers and off-pool deals
    Someone can send loud tokens to a custom contract with plain ft_transfer and trade claims on them, sell the account itself, or sell over the counter. The tag still follows the tokens: whoever ends up with them holds loud tokens and faces the same door. LOUD limits the pool exit and has no say over side deals.
    Other pools
    Loud tokens cannot enter other contracts through ft_transfer_call, so standard DEX deposits reject them. Quiet tokens can trade anywhere.
    First come, first served
    Ready exits share the cap in the order their leave calls land. A large holder can take a whole window's room. Splitting gives no extra room, and it gives nobody else priority either.
    Early honest buyers are loud too
    Anyone who buys in the launch window accepts the door.
    Notice can trigger a rush
    Quiet holders can sell before a shouted exit becomes ready. That is the intended information, and it can also accelerate a sell-off.
    No identity
    The contract does not know who is an insider and does not try to.
    Sybil resistance
    The cap is global, so more accounts never mean more exit room. That is the whole sybil defence; there is no per-person fairness.
    The creator allocation is unbacked
    It is minted without a NEAR payment, so every NEAR it receives through the door comes from buyers. After the creator has left, the pool can hold less NEAR than the formula price of the remaining tokens. sell fails with E_POOL_EMPTY when the pool cannot pay the formula amount, and leave fills only the part the pool can pay. So the last sellers may be unable to sell part or all of their tokens.
    Testnet simplifications
    The contract pays account storage out of the same balance that holds the pool's NEAR. Dust transfers to many fresh accounts can block sell and leave payouts until someone sends NEAR to the token account. The code is unaudited. There is no fee and no liquidity migration.

    Parameters

    Fixed at deployment with no setter. Anyone can read them with get_params. The values below are the standard preset.

    ParameterStandardWhat it is for, and what it costs
    Supply1,000,000,000Minted once. There is no mint method, so a cap written as a share of supply stays the same number of tokens.
    Creator allocation5%Paid to the creator as loud tokens, so the creator uses the same door as everyone else. At most 20%. A larger allocation means a longer queue at the door.
    Virtual NEAR reserve100 NEARSets the starting price and how much the first buys move it. Higher means a calmer start and fewer tokens per NEAR for the first buyers.
    Launch window10 minBuys in this window are loud. It covers the minutes when bundles and snipers buy before most people can react. A longer window tags more ordinary buyers as well.
    Notice30 minWait between shout and the first possible leave. Long enough for a person to see the board and act, short enough that an early buyer is not locked in for hours.
    Exit window60 minHow long a shout stays usable after the notice. It stops old shouts from sitting on the board as free options. Too short, and a ready exit can expire before the cap has room for it.
    Cap0.5% per 60 minSize of the bucket and its refill per window, for all accounts together. The bucket starts empty at launch and never holds more than one cap, so a single window lets out at most the room already in the bucket plus one refill. A holder of 5% needs about 10 hours to leave. A lower cap slows a dump more and makes early buyers wait longer in the same line.

    Bounds are checked once at deployment: creator allocation 0 to 20%, cap 0.01% to 10% of supply per window, launch window 1 min to 7 days, notice 30 s to 7 days, exit window and cap window 1 min to 7 days. The standard values are the ones written in the specification. The simulation report in the repository (sim/RESULTS.md) checks them against tighter and looser variants and states where LOUD does worse than no rule.
